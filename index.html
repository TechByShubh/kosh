What payment apps leave out

  • No budget to measure againstA list of payments is only history until there is a plan to compare it with.
  • No family viewHouseholds share money but are tracked as separate people. Nobody sees the total until it is too late.
  • No saving walletMoney set aside for a goal sits in the spending balance and is quietly spent.
  • No trustworthy place to learnLittle reliable news, and nowhere to ask an ordinary question.
  • No separate token identityRewards are tied to the very account that holds the money.
  • No adviserThe data needed for useful advice is already in the app. Nothing is done with it.

Apps are very good at recording money and very poor at helping people understand it, and the gap is widest for a family, where money is shared but visibility is not.

In a payment app, a transaction stops once it is recorded. In Kosh it goes through four more stages, and each one gives the family something the stage before could not.

What payment apps doWhat Kosh adds

Seven things, one family

Smart budget tracking

Every payment is sorted into food, transport, education, entertainment and the rest, using four kinds of evidence, so a budget can be measured without anyone labelling purchases by hand.

Correct a category once and every payment to that shop follows it.

In the appPress and hold any payment to see why it got its category, and change it.

Family tracking and planning

Members join a family space and each decides what the others may see: transactions, budgets, saving goals. Nothing is shared automatically, and any of it can be withdrawn at any time.

Together the family agrees a monthly budget, watches it as the month runs, and talks about it in the same place.

Four rolesAccount owner, family administrator, family member, and a restricted role for younger children.

A saving wallet

Money for a goal is kept apart from spending money, so it is not used for something else on the way. A ₹6,000 goal saved at ₹500 a week is reached in week twelve, and Kosh keeps that date honest as the pace changes.

In the appSlide to save, plan a weekly amount, or freeze a goal.

Awareness and community

Short lessons, a glossary, live headlines from a real financial newspaper, and a moderated place to ask an ordinary question about money.

Nothing inventedNews is read live from the publisher's own feed. Offline, the app says so.

A separate token identity

Rewards for keeping a budget, saving every week or finishing a lesson are held in a token identity of their own, apart from the account that holds money. Tokens can be redeemed, never spent or sent.

A personal adviser

It reads what the other parts have collected and says, in plain words and with the figures beside it, what is happening and what will happen if nothing changes. It never moves money and never decides for you.

Minimum necessary dataEach answer says exactly what it was allowed to read, such as "Entertainment total and budget, this month only".

Private by design

Financial data says more about a person than almost anything else, so security is the condition Kosh exists under, not a feature added at the end.

Permission, never assumed

A family member sees exactly what the owner has allowed, no more, and the owner can withdraw it at any time.

An access log

Every view of shared data and every change is written down: who, what and when.

App lock

A four-digit PIN, typed twice to set, asked for every time Kosh opens.

Roles

A restricted member sees their own budget and the family totals, never another person's payments.

The adviser's limits

It reads only the figures a question needs, and shows which ones.

Verified copies only

Every copy of the app must be verified at the Kosh stall before it opens, so a copied APK is useless.
